\chapter{Results}

\section{Experimental Results}

This chapter reports the classification results obtained from high frequency partial discharge (PD) signals acquired from medium voltage overhead lines. Two optimization regimes are compared in terms of their selected feature sets and the resulting classification performance a Featurewiz-based selection track and an MLJAR track. Model performance is reported using Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-score, and ROC AUC, with results summarized in the tables and figures that follow.

Table \ref{tab:fs_comparison_outcomes} lists the 30 features that were jointly selected, providing a direct view of the overlap between the Featurewiz and MLJAR selections. The prevalence of both original descriptors and interaction terms among the jointly selected features indicates that both regimes consistently retained information reflecting amplitude characteristics, spectral content, phase-resolved distribution structure, and signal complexity.

\subsection{Top-Ranked Features Under Each Selection Track}

Figure~\ref{fig:featurewiz_xgb_sig} presents the top ten features ranked by ANOVA F-score under the Featurewiz--XGBoost track. The ranking is dominated by \textit{spectral\_entropy}, followed by frequency-domain interaction and entropy-combination terms, including \textit{fft\_mean\_div\_stft\_mean} and \textit{shannon\_entropy\_plus\_spectral\_entropy}. Classical time-domain statistics such as \textit{kurtosis} appear within the top ten but are ranked below entropy- and FFT/STFT-derived descriptors.

Figure~\ref{fig:mljar_sig} shows the corresponding top ten ranking produced by the MLJAR internal feature selection track. In contrast to the Featurewiz track, MLJAR prioritizes composite and interaction variables dominated by combinations of \textit{spectral\_entropy} with \textit{fractal\_dimension}, RMS-related terms, and PRPD-related ratios. Notably, \textit{spectral\_entropy} and its derived combinations appear consistently among the highest-ranked features across both tracks, indicating stable retention of entropy-based descriptors despite differences in the specific formulations emphasized by each regime.

Overall, the top-ranked features from both tracks show agreement at the level of feature families (entropy/complexity and frequency-domain summaries), while differing in the relative emphasis placed on standalone descriptors versus composite interaction terms.

As summarized in Table \ref{tab:vsb_comparison}, recent partial discharge (PD) classification studies predominantly employ deep learning–based architectures, including convolutional neural networks and adversarial learning frameworks, to achieve high classification performance. The reported results indicate strong performance metrics, with accuracy values approaching or reaching near-perfect levels and high F1-scores, as observed in studies such as \parencite{Almehdhar_2025, Ishaq_2025, Kalaivani_2025, Li_2025_30}. These outcomes are largely attributed to the capability of deep learning models to automatically extract hierarchical and discriminative representations from complex PD signal patterns.

Despite their strong reported performance, the deep learning approaches summarized in the table rely on computationally intensive architectures, extensive data preprocessing, and large labeled datasets. Several studies further incorporate advanced techniques such as adversarial denoising, generative models, or deep feature augmentation to address challenges such as noise contamination and class imbalance. While these strategies enhance classification accuracy, they also increase model complexity and reduce transparency, which may pose challenges for practical deployment and physical interpretability in industrial PD monitoring applications.

In contrast, the proposed methodology adopts a classical machine learning framework based on a Random Forest classifier combined with automated feature selection across multiple PD domains. Although the achieved accuracy is lower than that reported by deep learning–based methods, the present work provides a more comprehensive and balanced evaluation through the simultaneous reporting of accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. Furthermore, the integration of time-domain, frequency-domain, statistical, and phase-resolved partial discharge (PRPD) features enables meaningful physical interpretation of the classification results. This positions the proposed approach as a transparent and computationally efficient alternative for PD diagnosis, particularly in scenarios where data availability, interpretability, and deployment constraints are critical considerations.
