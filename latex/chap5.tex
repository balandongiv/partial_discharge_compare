\chapter{Discussion}

\section{Overview of Key Findings}

The experimental results presented in Chapter 4 demonstrate that partial discharge (PD) classification performance is fundamentally determined by the synergistic interaction between feature representation strategies and the inherent inductive biases of the evaluated machine learning classifiers \parencite{Aldosari_2023, Srivastava_2023}. Across both feature selection tracks, a consistent subset of multi-domain descriptors emerged as critical discriminators, encompassing time-domain amplitude statistics, frequency and time--frequency spectral summaries, phase-resolved distribution measures expressed as PRPD quadrant contrasts, and signal complexity indicators \parencite{Zhang_2024_28, Seitz_2024}. This convergence in feature selection outcomes aligns with established literature suggesting that multi-domain representations effectively preserve complementary information about pulse morphology, spectral structure, and phase-resolved discharge behaviour \parencite{Long_2024, Orellana_2023}.

The utilization of long-term field monitoring data from multiple European sites provides a realistic assessment framework that captures the substantial variability inherent in in-situ PD measurements due to diverse operating and environmental conditions \parencite{Sikorski_2025, Florkowski_2024}. The observed performance ranking differences across models—where ANN achieved the highest F1 score (0.5103) on Track C while SVM achieved the highest accuracy (91.59\%) and ROC-AUC (0.8998)—are most appropriately interpreted as metric- and risk-dependent trade-offs rather than as universally dominant algorithmic behaviour \parencite{Zemouri_2023, Adhikari_2024}. This interpretation is particularly relevant given the class imbalance present in the dataset (approximately 89\% no-fault versus 11\% fault instances), which necessitates careful consideration of precision--recall trade-offs in operational deployment scenarios.

\section{Discussion Based on Research Objectives}

\subsection{Objective 1: Comparative evaluation of traditional machine learning algorithms}

The first research objective required a systematic, controlled comparison of conventional machine learning classifiers for PD detection and classification under standardized experimental conditions \parencite{Aldosari_2023, Srivastava_2023}. The reported results establish an evidence-based performance ordering of algorithm behaviour under a common feature engineering pipeline and comparable cross-validation protocol, addressing a critical gap in the existing PD diagnostics literature \parencite{Sikorski_2025, Amaya_Sanchez_2024}. This type of controlled benchmarking is frequently recommended in PD diagnostics research because performance can otherwise be dominated by confounding factors such as differences in sensing modality, preprocessing strategies, and evaluation protocols \parencite{Sikorski_2025, Vera_2023}.

The observed competitiveness of margin-based classifiers (SVM) and ensemble methods (Random Forest) is consistent with their widespread adoption in PD pattern recognition studies utilizing engineered feature representations \parencite{Aldosari_2023, Govindarajan_2021_2}. Specifically, SVM demonstrated superior accuracy (91.59\%) and ROC-AUC (0.8998) on Track C, which can be attributed to its ability to construct optimal separating hyperplanes in high-dimensional feature spaces while maintaining good generalization properties \parencite{Govindarajan_2021_2, Aldosari_2023}. Conversely, the relative underperformance of simpler baseline methods such as Decision Tree and k-Nearest Neighbors aligns with literature indicating that PD signatures often exhibit nonlinear and nonstationary characteristics under realistic noise conditions, necessitating more sophisticated learning algorithms \parencite{Friebe_Jenau_2024, Long_2024}.

The superior F1 score performance of Artificial Neural Networks (0.5103 on Track C, 0.4842 on Track B) suggests that these nonlinear learners can effectively exploit higher-order feature interactions and complex decision boundaries that may not be captured by linear or ensemble methods \parencite{Aldosari_2023, Srivastava_2023}. However, this performance advantage must be balanced against the increased computational complexity and reduced interpretability compared to more traditional classifiers, which are important considerations for real-time monitoring applications \parencite{Klein_2024, Sikorski_2025}.

\subsection{Objective 2: Identification of optimal feature sets via feature selection}

The second research objective focused on identifying informative feature subsets that maximize classification performance while maintaining computational efficiency \parencite{Zhang_2024_28, Seitz_2024}. The substantial overlap in jointly retained descriptors between Featurewiz (Track B, 187 features) and MLJAR (Track C, 925 features) selection regimes indicates that both methods converged on feature families that are routinely recognized as informative for PD characterization in the literature \parencite{Long_2024, Aldosari_2023}. This convergence provides strong evidence for the stability and interpretability of the selected feature representations.

Time-domain amplitude statistics (RMS, skewness, kurtosis, crest factor) and impulsiveness summaries were consistently retained across both tracks, which is consistent with reports that these descriptors effectively capture the intermittent high-amplitude transient behaviour characteristic of partial discharge pulses \parencite{Choudhary_2024, Agarwal_2023}. Frequency and time--frequency descriptors (FFT mean/max, STFT mean/max, spectral entropy) were similarly prioritized, reflecting their utility in characterizing PD energy distribution across frequency bands and their robustness to interference and signal propagation effects \parencite{Friebe_Jenau_2024, Bao_2024_2}. PRPD-derived distribution summaries, particularly quadrant-based differences and polarity ratios, were extensively retained, supporting their established role in compactly encoding phase-locked discharge behaviour into interpretable statistical measures \parencite{Orellana_2023, Seitz_2024}.

The modest incremental performance gain observed from the larger feature set (F1 score improvement from 0.4842 to 0.5103, representing a 5.4\% relative increase) can be interpreted through the lens of feature redundancy and noise sensitivity \parencite{Klein_2024, Long_2024}. While Track C's expanded feature space (925 features) enabled slightly better discrimination, particularly in recall performance (42.85\% versus 38.37\%), the substantial increase in dimensionality (approximately 5-fold) introduces computational overhead and potential overfitting risks that must be carefully managed in deployment scenarios \parencite{Srivastava_2023, Sikorski_2025}. This observation aligns with the principle of diminishing returns in feature expansion, where additional features may contribute marginal information while increasing model complexity and vulnerability to noise \parencite{Long_2024, Klein_2024}.

\subsection{Objective 3: Assessment of model generalizability}

The third research objective addressed the critical question of model generalizability and robustness under realistic field conditions \parencite{Sikorski_2025, Klein_2024}. The evidence reported in Chapter 4 provides within-dataset robustness validation under a rigorous 5-fold stratified cross-validation protocol applied to long-term monitoring data spanning multiple sites and temporal periods \parencite{Srivastava_2023, Florkowski_2024}. This evaluation framework aligns with literature emphasizing the importance of testing diagnostic models under realistic field variability rather than exclusively under controlled laboratory conditions \parencite{Sikorski_2025, Vera_2023}.

However, it is crucial to recognize that generalizability across fundamentally different datasets, sensing configurations, geographic regions, or asset populations cannot be conclusively established from within-dataset validation alone \parencite{Seitz_2024, Raymond_2022_1}. Prior research has consistently emphasized that domain shift may arise from systematic differences in noise statistics, sensor placement and bandwidth characteristics, asset-specific electromagnetic propagation paths, and environmental conditions \parencite{Friebe_Jenau_2024, Orellana_2023}. Accordingly, the present findings are most appropriately interpreted as a reference benchmark for the specific data context studied—European overhead line monitoring with contactless antenna sensing—rather than as definitive evidence of cross-domain transferability \parencite{Sikorski_2025, Srivastava_2023}.

\section{Interpretation of Model Performance and Feature Characteristics}

A fundamental pattern emerging from the experimental results is the clear separation between overall classification correctness (accuracy) and balanced error trade-offs (F1 score), which reflects the inherent challenges of imbalanced diagnostic datasets \parencite{Zemouri_2023, Srivastava_2023}. This behaviour is consistent with well-established limitations of accuracy as a performance metric under class imbalance, where models can achieve high accuracy by predominantly predicting the majority class while failing to detect critical minority class instances \parencite{Adhikari_2024, Zemouri_2023}. In the present study, despite achieving accuracy values exceeding 91\% across multiple models, recall values remained relatively modest (ranging from 20.71\% to 42.85\%), indicating that a substantial proportion of actual fault instances were not correctly identified.

For PD monitoring applications, recall-oriented performance interpretation is often emphasized because missed detections (false negatives) can have severe risk implications in condition monitoring contexts, potentially leading to progressive insulation degradation and unexpected equipment failures \parencite{Adhikari_2024, Hussain_2021_1}. The observed precision--recall trade-offs, where Random Forest achieved the highest precision (76.70\% on Track B) but the lowest recall (23.54\%), versus ANN achieving the best recall (42.85\% on Track C) with moderate precision (64.65\%), illustrate the fundamental tension between minimizing false alarms and maximizing fault detection sensitivity \parencite{Zemouri_2023, Adhikari_2024}.

The persistence of similar jointly selected features across both feature selection tracks provides strong evidence for the stability and physical interpretability of the engineered descriptors \parencite{Seitz_2024, Zhang_2024_28}. Time--frequency descriptors and entropy-like measures (spectral entropy, Shannon entropy combinations) were consistently prioritized, which aligns with their established role in reflecting spectral complexity and signal irregularity under noise and interference conditions \parencite{Bao_2024_2, Friebe_Jenau_2024}. Phase-resolved PRPD summaries, particularly quadrant-based differences and polarity ratios, were extensively retained, supporting their interpretation as proxies for discharge phase preference and polarity asymmetry under phase-locked AC excitation \parencite{Orellana_2023, Seitz_2024}.

The superior balanced performance of neural network classifiers (ANN achieving F1 scores of 0.5103 and 0.4842 on Tracks C and B, respectively) can be attributed to their ability to exploit higher-order feature interactions and nonlinear decision boundaries in the engineered representations \parencite{Aldosari_2023, Srivastava_2023}. However, such performance advantages are typically conditional on careful hyperparameter tuning, regularization strategies, and validation protocols in noisy monitoring settings, as evidenced by the relatively high standard deviations observed in precision and recall metrics \parencite{Srivastava_2023, Klein_2024}.

The strong discrimination capability demonstrated by SVM classifiers, achieving the highest ROC-AUC (0.8998) and accuracy (91.59\%) on Track C, is consistent with their frequent application in PD diagnostics with high-dimensional feature spaces, where their margin-maximization objective provides robust generalization \parencite{Govindarajan_2021_2, Aldosari_2023}. Ensemble tree models (Random Forest) exhibited precision-forward behaviour, achieving the highest precision values (76.70\% on Track B, 71.69\% on Track C) but substantially lower recall, which may be interpreted as an operational trade-off favouring high-confidence predictions at the expense of detection sensitivity \parencite{Zemouri_2023, Adhikari_2024}.

\section{Comparison with Existing Literature}

The benchmark comparison presented in Chapter 4 must be interpreted with appropriate caution, as reported performance metrics across different studies are often not directly comparable due to fundamental differences in experimental design, data characteristics, and evaluation protocols \parencite{Aldosari_2023, Sikorski_2025}. Differences in sensing modality (contactless antenna versus galvanic coupling, HFCT, or UHF sensors), defect taxonomy and classification granularity, and evaluation methodology (cross-validation strategy, class balance, label quality) are widely cited as primary drivers of apparent performance discrepancies in the PD diagnostics literature \parencite{Vera_2023, Long_2024}.

Studies emphasizing cable terminal PD signals, GIS-specific discharge patterns, or PRPD-image-based classification pipelines may leverage data structures and feature representations that differ substantially from the antenna-based long-term monitoring signals utilized in the present work \parencite{Cai_2025, Sian_2023}. Moreover, studies reporting high accuracy values (ranging from 91.5\% to 97.0\% in the cited literature) may differ materially in class balance, label quality and annotation consistency, noise cancellation assumptions, and the specific fault types included in the classification task \parencite{Zemouri_2023, Friebe_Jenau_2024}. The present study's best accuracy of 91.59\% (SVM on Track C) positions these results within the lower-to-mid range of reported values, which is reasonable given the realistic field monitoring context and class imbalance challenges.

Accordingly, the present results are most appropriately positioned as an interpretable baseline within a multi-domain feature engineering framework and conventional machine learning model zoo, rather than as a direct performance comparison against studies utilizing fundamentally different data acquisition and processing methodologies \parencite{Aldosari_2023, Srivastava_2023}. The continued recommendation to report multiple complementary performance metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, ROC-AUC) is consistent with established diagnostic evaluation guidance in imbalanced classification settings, where single-metric optimization can lead to misleading conclusions about practical utility \parencite{Zemouri_2023, Adhikari_2024}.

\section{Strengths of the Proposed Approach}

The utilization of long-term, multi-site field monitoring data from European overhead power lines represents a significant strength of this research, aligning with literature that emphasizes realism and environmental variability in deployment-oriented PD analytics \parencite{Sikorski_2025, Klein_2024}. This data acquisition strategy provides a more realistic assessment framework compared to laboratory-based studies, capturing the inherent variability in PD signals due to diverse operating conditions, weather patterns, and temporal degradation effects \parencite{Florkowski_2024, Orellana_2023}. The multi-year temporal scope (nearly three years) and geographic distribution (nine monitoring stations across Czechia and Slovakia) enable robust evaluation of model stability under heterogeneous field conditions.

The comprehensive multi-domain feature engineering strategy, encompassing time-domain statistics, frequency-domain spectral analysis, time--frequency representations (STFT), and phase-resolved PRPD measures, is consistent with established recommendations to combine complementary signal descriptors for enhanced PD separability \parencite{Long_2024, Aldosari_2023}. This approach recognizes that different PD types and fault mechanisms manifest distinct characteristics across multiple signal domains, necessitating a holistic feature representation to achieve robust classification performance.

The convergence and agreement between two independent feature selection regimes (Featurewiz and MLJAR) provides an internal stability signal that is often recommended when interpreting feature relevance in high-dimensional settings, where selection algorithms may exhibit sensitivity to initialization or optimization criteria \parencite{Seitz_2024, Zhang_2024_28}. The substantial overlap in jointly selected features (as detailed in Table \ref{tab:fs_comparison_outcomes} in Chapter 4) strengthens confidence in the physical interpretability and diagnostic relevance of the retained descriptors.

The comprehensive evaluation using multiple complementary performance metrics supports risk-aware interpretation, which is emphasized in condition monitoring and diagnostic classification literature where different operational contexts may prioritize different aspects of model performance \parencite{Adhikari_2024, Zemouri_2023}. By reporting accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, and ROC-AUC with associated uncertainty estimates, the present work enables informed decision-making based on application-specific risk tolerance and operational constraints.

\section{Limitations of the Study}

The experimental results primarily provide within-dataset evidence under the stated cross-validation protocol and therefore do not establish transferability across substantially different acquisition setups, asset populations, or geographic regions \parencite{Seitz_2024, Raymond_2022_1}. Domain shift associated with sensor bandwidth characteristics, installation geometry and coupling mechanisms, and environmental interference patterns is widely discussed as a fundamental deployment limitation in PD monitoring systems \parencite{Sikorski_2025, Friebe_Jenau_2024}.

A critical limitation concerns the geographic and climatic context of the training data. The dataset utilized in this study was acquired from monitoring stations located in Czechia and Slovakia, regions characterized by temperate continental climates with distinct four-season annual cycles (spring, summer, autumn, winter) \parencite{Florkowski_2024, Sikorski_2025}. This climatic variability introduces specific environmental conditions—including temperature fluctuations, humidity patterns, precipitation regimes, and seasonal insulation stress variations—that may not be representative of other geographic regions with different climate characteristics.

Specifically, the four-season climate pattern in Central Europe may influence PD activity through mechanisms such as seasonal temperature-dependent insulation properties, humidity-driven surface contamination effects, and weather-related electromagnetic interference patterns \parencite{Florkowski_2024, Orellana_2023}. The generalizability of models trained on this data to regions with fundamentally different climate regimes, such as tropical or equatorial climates with only two distinct seasons (wet and dry), remains unvalidated. For instance, deployment in Malaysia, which experiences a tropical climate with two primary seasons rather than four, may encounter different PD signal characteristics, environmental noise patterns, and insulation stress profiles that could affect model performance \parencite{Raymond_2022_1, Azam_2024}. This limitation highlights the importance of region-specific validation and potential domain adaptation strategies when deploying PD classification models across different geographic and climatic contexts.

The presence of precision--recall performance gaps, where even the best-performing models achieved recall values below 43\%, indicates that sensitivity to the minority fault class remains limited, which is consistent with known challenges under class imbalance and overlapping discharge signatures \parencite{Zemouri_2023, Aldosari_2023}. This constraint motivates the need for threshold calibration, cost-sensitive learning strategies, and class rebalancing techniques when false-negative risk is operationally salient in condition monitoring applications \parencite{Adhikari_2024, Zemouri_2023}.

The substantial feature-set size difference across tracks (187 versus 925 features) reflects a fundamental complexity--maintainability trade-off that is frequently discussed for on-line monitoring and edge deployment scenarios, where computational resources, memory constraints, and real-time processing requirements may favour more compact feature representations \parencite{Klein_2024, Sikorski_2025}. While the larger feature set (Track C) achieved marginally better performance, the 5-fold increase in dimensionality introduces significant computational overhead and potential overfitting risks that must be carefully managed in practical deployment contexts.

\section{Practical and Engineering Implications}

Model selection for PD monitoring applications is most defensibly framed as an application-specific decision rather than as a single-metric optimization problem, where the choice of algorithm and feature set should be guided by operational requirements, risk tolerance, and deployment constraints \parencite{Vera_2023, Sikorski_2025}. Where missed PD detections are prioritized as high-risk outcomes that could lead to catastrophic equipment failure or safety hazards, recall-sensitive operating points are commonly emphasized in condition monitoring discussions, favouring models such as ANN that demonstrated superior recall performance (42.85\%) \parencite{Adhikari_2024, Hussain_2021_1}.

Conversely, where false alarms impose substantial operational costs through unnecessary maintenance interventions, equipment shutdowns, or resource allocation, higher-precision operating points may be preferred, subject to explicit risk management protocols \parencite{Zemouri_2023, Sikorski_2025}. In such scenarios, Random Forest models achieving precision values exceeding 76\% may be operationally advantageous despite their lower recall performance.

The interpretability of engineered features, particularly time-domain statistics, frequency-domain descriptors, and PRPD measures, can support engineering acceptance and regulatory compliance when model outputs must be reconciled with established PD assessment procedures and industry standards \parencite{Aldosari_2023, Vera_2023}. This interpretability advantage may favour feature-based approaches over deep learning methods that utilize raw signal representations, despite potential performance trade-offs.

Resource-aware deployment considerations, including computational complexity, memory requirements, and real-time processing capabilities, are frequently highlighted for continuous monitoring and edge computing scenarios \parencite{Klein_2024, Sikorski_2025}. The Featurewiz track (187 features) may be preferable for edge deployment where computational resources are constrained, despite the marginal performance advantage of the larger MLJAR feature set (925 features), which requires approximately 5-fold greater computational resources.

\section{Transition to Future Work}

External validation across additional assets, monitoring sites, and acquisition modalities remains a primary requirement for establishing broader transferability and generalizability of the developed models \parencite{Seitz_2024, Sikorski_2025}. This validation should specifically include testing on datasets from different geographic regions and climate regimes to assess the impact of environmental variability on model performance, addressing the limitation identified regarding four-season versus two-season climate contexts \parencite{Raymond_2022_1, Azam_2024}.

Domain adaptation and robustness testing are frequently motivated when noise statistics, electromagnetic coupling paths, and environmental interference patterns differ across deployment contexts \parencite{Friebe_Jenau_2024, Raymond_2022_1}. Techniques such as transfer learning, domain adversarial training, and data augmentation strategies that simulate diverse environmental conditions could enhance model robustness across different geographic and climatic regions.

In parallel, approaches that explicitly address class imbalance and asymmetric risk should be evaluated using calibrated decision thresholds, cost-sensitive learning objectives, and advanced sampling techniques (e.g., SMOTE, ADASYN) to improve minority class detection sensitivity \parencite{Zemouri_2023, Adhikari_2024}. Data augmentation and advanced denoising strategies are also frequently discussed as practical methods for improving robustness under interference and enhancing model generalization \parencite{Bao_2024_2, Friebe_Jenau_2024}.

Finally, a principled reduction of expanded feature sets alongside interpretability analysis (e.g., SHAP values, feature importance rankings) and uncertainty quantification can be pursued to support deployable monitoring workflows that balance performance, computational efficiency, and engineering trust \parencite{Klein_2024, Vera_2023}. The integration of explainable AI techniques with the developed models would further enhance their practical utility in industrial deployment scenarios where diagnostic interpretability is critical for maintenance decision-making.
